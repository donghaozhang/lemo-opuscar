# 《最后一场皮影戏》 — Treatment

Style: Chinese Shadow Puppetry (`styles/shadow-puppet/STYLE.md`, fixed). Language: Simplified Chinese for every title,
placard and the `.srt`. 140 s, 1920×1080, 24 fps. No voice-over, no singing. Score: gong-and-drum theatre. Mix −14 LUFS,
true peak ≤ −1 dBTP. Owner's material: none. Everything is drawn with code. Storyboard review: none; this document holds
every decision.

## 0. The brief, summed up

A village square at night, the last performance of an old shadow-puppet master before he retires. His grandson, who
would rather play on his phone, is drafted as his helper behind the screen. The show is *Monkey King fights the White
Bone Demon*. Halfway through the old man's hands shake (planted early) and the Monkey King puppet falls; for a moment
the puppets on the screen carry on by themselves (the one piece of magic) while the demon gains the upper hand, until
the grandson grabs the rods and, clumsily at first and then with flair, brings the Monkey King back to win. When the
oil lamp gutters, the boy lights the screen with his phone (set-up and pay-off). The crowd cheers; the old man hands
the boy the lamp. Dawn: the boy practises alone with the Monkey King.

## 1. Three candidate structures

**A. Chronological, one night.** Lamp lit → square → show → crisis → comeback → lamp handed over → dawn. One line,
one night, the lamp as the through-object. Risk: predictable; the strength is that every set-up pays off in order
and the climax has room.

**B. Bookend from the dawn.** Open on the boy alone at dawn with the Monkey King, then "the night before". Risk: it
gives away the ending and spends the quiet dawn image as a hook instead of as the reward.

**C. Two screens, front and back only.** Strict alternation: every front shot (the legend in colour) answered by a
back shot (the hands that make it), no square, no audience. Pure and rigorous, but the brief wants wides of the square
and the crowd, and the cheer needs a crowd.

**Chosen: A, with C's discipline inside the show.** One night, chronological, but the show section is cut front /
back / front with match cuts between a puppet's gesture and the hand that makes it, and the crowd is a character
(three reactions: laugh, gasp, cheer). The lamp opens the film (a match, then the lamp), is handed over at the end of
the night, and the sun replaces it at dawn: first light, the gift of light, daylight.

**The one big decision: the whole film is a shadow play.** Not only the legend: the square, the master, the boy and the
audience are also carved, dyed hide pressed on a cloth and lit from behind. The "real" world is a wide, dim cloth lit
by two low night lamps (indigo, jade, ochre, raw hide, ink); the show inside it is a second, small, bright cloth with
its own oil lamp (vermilion, gold, malachite). Backstage is the real world's puppets in profile in front of the
mirrored show cloth. This keeps one rendering model for every frame (`lamp × transmission × cloth`), makes night dark
without any blue, and makes the match cuts literal: a hand piece on a rod, a rod to a rivet, a puppet on the cloth.

**Expressions.** The style says a puppet's face never changes, and the show puppets obey that. The master and the boy
need real eye acting (brief), so their faces use the style's own device, the **piece swap and the slide on a rod**:
each has an open-cut face with a brow piece that turns on a rivet, an eyelid piece that slides down, a pupil piece
that slides inside the eye hole, and a mouth strip that is swapped (line, smile, open, grim, downturned). Together
with head angle and body tilt that gives six clearly different expressions, all made of pieces that rotate, slide or
swap, never morph. Checked on the expression sheet before any shot is animated.

## 2. Logline and arc

**Logline.** On the night of his last show, an old shadow master's hands fail him mid-fight, and the grandson who only
wanted his phone picks up the rods, saves the Monkey King, and lights the screen with the one thing he was told to put
away.

**Setup.** A match, a lamp, a jug with the last of its oil, a trembling hand shaking out the match. The boy in the
cold glow of his phone. The square fills. The boy is drafted; the phone goes in the pocket; he is given the cloud.

**Turn.** The hands shake; the Monkey King falls off the cloth. For four seconds the shadows move with nobody holding
them, and the demon grows huge against the lamp. The boy grabs the rods.

**Ending.** Clumsy, then the somersault his grandfather taught him with one tap, then the staff pulled to the lamp
until its shadow fills the screen. The lamp gutters; the phone lights the cloth; the crowd cheers; the lamp changes
hands. Dawn: the boy alone, the Monkey King raised against a cloth lit by the sun.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Lotte Reiniger, *Prince Achmed* (grammar only):** profile acting carried by silhouette and timing, scenery as lace
  on empty light, cutting between scales of figure. *Learn:* how a profile head turn carries a whole emotion; how a
  held pose reads. *Don't take:* any figure, any design.
- **Shaanxi / Huayin shadow theatre (grammar only):** the carving density, dye on hide, the 锣鼓经 (gong-and-drum
  patterns) that make the camera, the freeze on an accent. *Learn:* one hit per visual step, a gong per decisive
  strike, the accelerating roll into an entrance. *Don't take:* any tune, any recorded pattern, any named puppet.
- **Late-night theatre films (*Opening Night*, *Birdman*: grammar only):** the cut between the stage and the wings,
  faces in the wings lit by the stage. *Learn:* the wings as the real drama; the audience as a chorus. *Don't take:*
  shots or story.

## 4. Characters and sets (drawn in code)

- **爷爷, the master.** Thin, bent, a long grey beard on three riveted strips (pendulum), a felt skullcap, an indigo
  padded jacket with raw-hide sleeves, big hands. Open-cut face, swap-able expression pieces. Holds two rods in the
  right hand (Monkey) and one in the left (the demon's neck rod) when he works both.
- **小满, the boy, about ten.** Round head, a cowlick tuft (pendulum), a jade-green jacket with an ochre scarf, short
  legs, a phone (a small dark hide piece with a bright carved screen hole, lit by its own cool lamp). Open-cut face,
  swap-able expression pieces.
- **孙悟空, the Monkey King.** Open-cut face with one large gold-rimmed eye hole (the golden eye), a phoenix cap with
  two long pheasant feathers (pendulums), gold armour, vermilion cape and skirt, tiger-stripe kilt, a tail (chain
  pendulum), the staff as a separate piece on the hand line.
- **白骨精, the demon.** Form 1 a village maiden: a *solid* pretty face (the tell), a malachite dress, long ribbons
  (pendulums), a basket of peaches. Form 2 the demon: a solid ink skull with hollow sockets, carved rib strips, an
  indigo tattered robe, claws. The swap is a head and sleeve swap on a cymbal crash.
- **The crowd.** Rows of ink heads seen from behind, a child on shoulders, a few hats; three states: still, laughing
  (heads bob), cheering (arms up). A dog at dawn.
- **Set F (front): the square at night.** Dark ground strip, roofs, a big tree, a lantern string (ochre lantern
  pieces, each a small lamp), the stage: a lacquered frame with the inner cloth glowing in the middle, the audience
  below. The same world, pushed in, becomes the show cloth.
- **Set B (back): behind the screen.** The inner cloth mirrored, a hanging oil lamp above it, the rail where rods
  rest, a bench with spare puppets and the oil jug, dark wood pillars, the night beyond.
- **Set D (dawn).** The square and the booth again, the night lamps replaced by one broad pale-gold lamp with no
  hotspot (the sun behind the cloth), the stage lamp out.

Dyes: vermilion, gold/ochre, malachite/jade, indigo; raw hide; ink. Night = two dim lamps; the show = one bright lamp
(≈ 6 : 1 hotspot); the phone = one small cool-white lamp; dawn = one wide lamp, no hotspot.
