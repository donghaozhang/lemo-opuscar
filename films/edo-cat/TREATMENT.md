# TREATMENT — 《江户猫追鱼记》 (The Edo Cat and the Fish)

Ukiyo-e, 1920×1080, 24 fps, ≈ 140 s, no voice. On-screen text in Simplified Chinese. Written before opening the
style's `DEMO.md` or demo code.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **The handscroll chase (chosen).** The whole film is one long mounting scroll of prints. Each view of the chase is
   a complete print; the chase *runs out of one print, across the tan mount, into the next* while the camera slides
   right → left. The slides get shorter as the chase gets more desperate. Coverage (mediums, close-ups, inserts) is
   cut *inside* each print, so the scroll is the geography and the cuts are the acting. It bookends: the first and
   last prints are the same key block of the home bridge, printed as **dawn** and as **dusk** (alternate
   impressions).
2. **Alternate impressions of one street.** One key block of the fish-market street, reprinted for each phase (dawn,
   shower, snow…), with the chase looping round the same bridge. Strong formal idea, but the brief asks for distinct
   places (temple, rice fields, pass, sea), and one street can't hold a sea wave.
3. **A triptych unfolding.** Three sheets revealed one at a time: theft / chase / sea, the story readable only when
   they join. Elegant, but it squeezes a chase that wants many places and many cuts into three compositions.

Why 1: the chase *is* a journey, the handscroll is the medium's own way to travel, and "running across the mount
between prints" is a gag only this medium can do. The bookend lets the ending echo the opening exactly.

Opening image (from the topic, not the style's cliché): **an extreme close crop of two fish in a basket**, with a
scrawny paw creeping in — the two fish are the film's central set-up. Ending: the dusk bridge print washes back to
paper until only the red seal and the two fish shapes remain as a key line (see §8). Score shape: sneaky pizzicato →
stop → chase that accelerates → free-rhythm awe and a real silence → quiet koto lullaby.

## 2. Logline and arc

A starving stray cat steals a fish for the kitten it shelters under a bridge, loses it to a black kite, and chases
the bird across all of Edo — rain, snow, rice fields, the mountains, the sea — until it snatches the fish out of a
breaking wave and brings it home, where the fishmonger who chased it all day quietly leaves a second fish.

- **Setup** (0–38 s): dawn; the hungry kitten under the bridge; the fishmonger's basket with exactly **two** fish;
  the theft; the kite circling high (planted in the first wide); the snatch.
- **Turn / chase** (38–88 s): four views, each with one gag; the fishmonger always one view behind, suffering more.
- **Climax** (88–110 s): the sea. The kite drops the fish into a great wave; the cat leaps onto a fishing boat, rides
  up the wave, the music drops out, it leaps and catches the fish in its teeth. The spray floods the print to paper.
- **Ending** (110–140 s): dusk at the same bridge; the kitten eats; the fishmonger, soaked, looks down from the
  bridge, his anger melts, and he lays the second fish beside them. Lanterns, mist, the seal.

**Set-ups → pay-offs**
| Set-up (when) | Pay-off (when) |
|---|---|
| Insert: two fish in the basket (0 s); the paw takes one, the other stays (≈ 22 s) | The fishmonger lays the second fish (≈ 124 s) |
| Kitten under the bridge, hungry (8–13 s) | The kitten eats (≈ 116 s) |
| A small black kite circling in the dawn sky (3–8 s) | The snatch (≈ 33 s) |
| A fishing boat beached in the sea print's first wide (≈ 88 s) | The cat leaps onto it (≈ 92 s) |
| The fishmonger's yawn and indifference (≈ 19 s) | His soft, tired smile (≈ 121 s): the same face, other brows |
| The cat's lowered "determined" brows (≈ 13 s) | The cat's lifted, surprised brows at the second fish (≈ 127 s) |

## 3. Benchmark

- **Chuck Jones' chase cartoons (grammar only).** Learn: clear geography, a held anticipation before each dash, the
  "take" (a reaction close-up after each disaster), physical comedy that lands on the pursuer, gags that read at
  thumbnail size. Don't take: characters, designs, gags, music.
- **Utagawa Kuniyoshi's cat prints (public-domain prints, grammar only).** Learn: cats as flat silhouettes with
  personality, a few strokes for a face, tabby patches as flat colour blocks. Don't take: any composition or figure.
- **Hiroshige's station series / Hokusai's views (per STYLE.md).** Learn: tiny figures in big landscapes, rain lines,
  extreme foreground crops, high slanted field layers. Never copy a print — in particular the wave is seen from the
  shore side as a rising wall with one boat, no Fuji in the trough, no claw-foam copied.

## 4. Characters

- **The cat (野猫)**: scrawny, long-legged, ribs drawn as three key strokes, ragged ear notch, white body with two
  ochre/tabby patches (paper is the white), a long kinked tail. Eyes: almond shape, amber iris, black slit/round pupil,
  a heavy upper lid line and a separate brow tuft — so expressions read: *sly*, *determined*, *shocked*, *furious*,
  *awed*, *tender*, *surprised*.
- **The kitten (小猫)**: a round ball of grey with huge round eyes, a pink mouth; mews.
- **The fishmonger (鱼贩)**: stocky, indigo happi coat with a white wave crest, rope headband, shoulder pole (tenbin-bō)
  with two baskets; big expressive brows, a stubbly jaw. Faces: *yawning*, *double-take*, *furious*, *exhausted*,
  *soft smile*.
- **The kite (鸢)**: black-brown, forked tail, sharp yellow eye, smug. Claws for inserts.
- Extras: umbrella walkers, a straw-cape traveller, farmers in hats, a monk with a broom, fishermen.

## 9. Titles and subtitles

No voice, so no dialogue subtitles. All text is printed on the sheets:
- **View cartouches** (vertical cream slip, thick + thin border, pale tab "江户猫追鱼记", view title in a brush font,
  red seal): 一 晨市 · 二 骤雨 · 三 雪寺 · 四 稻田 · 五 山岭 · 六 怒涛 · 七 暮归.
- **One horizontal cartouche** for the fishmonger's shout "站——住！" (≈ 26 s) — the only "line" in the film.
- **End**: "终" in a cartouche beside the seal.
- The `.srt` carries the same texts (title, view names, the shout, the end) so the film reads with subtitles on.
- Fonts: Shippori Mincho (cartouche tabs, OFL) and Yuji Syuku (brush titles, OFL), subset to the characters used.

## 10. Native moves used

- **The handscroll** (all chase transitions, slides getting shorter).
- **The frame is an object** (the cat runs across the mount between prints; at the climax the fish and the cat fly
  out over the top border and the catch happens *in the mount* — the signature shot).
- **The handscroll** rolled back at speed for the way home.
- **Paper is the white** (snow, foam, rain-spray left as paper).
- **Alternate impressions** (the same bridge key block printed as dawn, then as dusk: the colour blocks change on the
  same key line).

## 11. Decisions taken without the owner

- Ending: fishmonger's gift is silent, no text. The kite is not punished (it just loses).
- Length ≈ 140 s, inside 100–150 s.
- Light: dawn pink-ochre (A, B), grey sumi shower, white-grey snow, bright midday fields, ochre afternoon pass, deep
  indigo sea, dusk indigo with lantern light (E): night/dusk grounds darker, lanterns as practical light.
