# 《墨龙借雨》 The Ink Dragon Borrows the Rain — treatment

Style: Chinese Ink Wash (`styles/ink-wash`). 1920×1080, 24 fps, 132 s. No voice. On-screen text, calligraphy and
`.srt` in Simplified Chinese. Score led by the guqin. Mix −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP.

Decisions the brief left to me are written here; nothing waits for approval.

## 1. Three candidate structures

**A. The incense clock (chosen).** A linear journey through the painted landscape, cross-cut three times with hard-cut
inserts of the incense stick in the studio, each shorter than the last. The deadline is a physical object we watch burn.
The studio bookends the film with the same wide framing, night and dawn.

**B. The handscroll.** One continuous right-to-left pan through the painting: the studio only at the head and tail of the
scroll, the whole journey one long painting. Elegant and very much the style's own signature, but it forbids the
cross-cutting that makes the deadline felt, and it drags the cloud argument (a static scene) into a moving camera.

**C. Dawn first.** Open on the painter waking to a rain-soaked painting and a wet fingertip, then tell the night as a
flashback. Loses the hook (the drop must fall in the first three seconds) and spends the ending in the first minute.

A wins because the story is a race against a burning stick: the audience must see the stick. One idea from B survives:
the journey through the mountains (S07) is a handscroll pan, right to left, with the dragon leading.

Opening image: not the painting, not the title: an ink drop swelling on the lip of the inkstone, trembling with each
snore. Ending: not a pull-back: one small mark, a curled dragon in the inkstone whose vermilion eye opens once and
closes, while the ink around it dries into paper. Score shape: free, unmetred guqin for the studio; a pulse once we are
inside the painting; two literal silences around the sacrifice; the dance in 宫 (major pentatonic) and a return to a
single harmonic for dawn.

## 2. Logline and arc

A drop of spilled ink becomes a small dragon and, before the painter's incense burns out, must bring rain to the
drought-struck village in the painting; when the only cloud refuses to wake, the dragon gives itself up and becomes
the rain.

- **Setup**: night, a sleeping painter, an unfinished painting of the painter's home village, dry. A drop falls. A
  dragon the size of a thumb comes alive when its vermilion eye opens (画龙点睛). It reads the colophon, sees the
  incense, understands the clock, and dives into the painting.
- **Turn**: the journey (mist, dry river, pine cliff) leads to a cloud asleep on the summit. Tapping, tugging, roaring:
  the cloud only snores, then blows the dragon off the cliff. The dragon hangs in the air and looks down at a child
  holding up an empty bowl. Silence. "If rain cannot be borrowed, be the rain."
- **Ending**: the dragon dives into the cloud as one flying-white stroke; its ink spreads inside the pale mass until the
  cloud, now heavy and dark, rains. The river fills, the boat floats, the bowl fills, the village dances. One vermilion
  drop falls last, out of the painting and into the inkstone. Dawn: the painter wakes, finds the painting wet, the
  colophon run, and writes the title 墨龙借雨 beside it; the seal. In the inkstone, a small dragon curls, asleep.

Set-ups and pay-offs: the incense (lit → half → ember → ash); the drip (opening → the one sound in the silence → the
red drop into the inkstone); the snore (painter → cloud → the small dragon at the end); the dry riverbed with a
beached boat → the boat afloat; the child's empty bowl → the bowl fills, the child drinks; the painter's colophon
「家山久旱，盼雨」 → the same colophon, run by rain, with 「墨龙借雨」 written beside it; the brush dropped from the sleeping
hand → picked up to write the title; the vermilion eye that gives life → the last drop → the dragon re-forms around it.

## 3. Benchmark

- **《山水情》 Feelings of Mountains and Water (Shanghai Animation Film Studio, 1988).** Learn: 70–80 % blank paper as
  space and silence, how a figure is three strokes, how the guqin breathes with the picture (plucks placed on actions,
  never looping), mist as the transition between places. Don't take: its story, its characters, its compositions, its
  music.
- **Chen Rong, 《九龙图》 Nine Dragons (1244).** Learn: how an ink dragon emerges from and dissolves into wet washes,
  the S-curve silhouette, the head read from three dark marks. Don't take: any dragon's pose or design.
- **Pixar, *Piper* (2016).** Learn: a small creature's eye acting against a large indifferent nature, how a gag reads
  from pose alone, anticipation before every move, the breath before the brave act. Don't take: character, design,
  shots, music.

## 4. The world

Two spaces, one drawing code. The **studio** is desk-space: inkstone, brush, brush-rest, seal-paste box, incense in
its holder, the sleeping painter, the painting unrolled on the desk. The **painting** is landscape-space: far ranges
(清), mist bands, a dry river with a beached boat, a withered tree, a village of six roofs, a pine cliff, and the summit
where the cloud sleeps. In the studio the painting is drawn small on the desk by the same landscape code; when the
dragon dives, the camera enters landscape-space and the painting fills the frame. One world → screen function per
space; every effect that follows the dragon goes through it.

**The dragon** (墨龙): thumb-sized next to the painter, dog-sized inside the painting. A spine of twelve points
following a path with a travelling sine; the body is one pale mass (淡) with two loaded strokes (浓) along the back,
a belly line, a mane and tail tuft in dry bristle strokes (飞白), two short horns, two whiskers, four small legs with
three-claw feet drawn only when it stands. Head: a dark muzzle mass, a brow stroke whose angle is the expression, an
eyelid stroke, and the **one vermilion eye** (the only accent in the world; it finds the dragon in wides and rhymes
with the seal). Figures step on twos; its position, blooms, ripples and splatter move on ones.

**The cloud** (云): a soft pale mass (清 → 淡), slow breathing scale, a face in four strokes: two closed eyelids, a brow,
a mouth that mumbles. It never leaves the summit. When inked it darkens through the five tones from inside out,
tide-lines growing.

**The painter**: a xieyi figure slumped on the desk: robe mass, head and bun, one arm, no face. At dawn: head lifts,
a hand touches the paper, a hand writes.

**Villagers**: body mass plus two loaded strokes, burnt-ink limbs; one child with a bowl held up.

## 9. Subtitles, inscriptions and title

- Inscriptions (题跋) are the film's only text, in **Ma Shan Zheng** (OFL brush regular script, from
  `@fontsource/ma-shan-zheng`), ink-coloured (浓), no box, a small vermilion square at the start of each line, placed in
  each shot's empty paper, vertical columns when the shot's blank space is tall, horizontal otherwise. They appear wet
  and dry in a fraction of a second. Hold ≥ 1.8 s and ≥ characters ÷ 4.5 + 1.5 s. Exported as `subtitles.srt`.
- Lines (7): 「家山久旱，盼雨。」(20.5–25.0, the painter's colophon on the painting) · 「香尽之前，须借得雨来。」(25.5–29.4) ·
  「云不肯醒。」(71.0–75.0) · 「借不来，便自己做雨。」(76.0–81.5) · 「雨来了。」(97.0–101.5) · 「墨龙借雨」(119.5–126.0, the title,
  written by the painter) · 「墨还在砚里。」(128.0–131.5, the last colophon, over the inkstone).
- The title 墨龙借雨 is calligraphy traced from the font skeleton in stroke order with pressure from glyph thickness,
  hidden-tip entries and tapered exits, written on the painting in a vertical column beside the colophon, world-anchored;
  the vermilion 白文 seal (lines knocked out of an irregular red square, ink-paste speckles) is pressed at 125.0. The seal
  reads 墨龙 (two characters).
- No LemoLab credit, no watermark: this is the owner's film.
